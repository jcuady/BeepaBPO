# Beepa BPO — Master Brandbook

---

# 1. Brand Purpose

Beepa exists to create meaningful progress through people and partnerships.

The company began from a deeply personal purpose: building something meaningful for a family's future. That purpose now extends to the Filipino professionals who find opportunities through Beepa and the clients who depend on Beepa to build capable, dependable teams.

Beepa must never be presented as simply a source of cheaper labor. The brand should communicate **trust, opportunity, capability, partnership, visibility, and sustainable growth**.

---

# 2. Brand Positioning

## Positioning Statement

> **Beepa is a people-first outsourcing partner helping businesses build dependable teams and grow with confidence.**

## Why This Positioning

It gives Beepa room to serve different industries and functions without becoming trapped as only a virtual-assistant provider, call center, or one-service outsourcing company.

Beepa should be positioned as an **outsourcing partner**, not only a staffing vendor.

---

# 3. Brand Promise

Beepa should consistently deliver five promises:

1. **Dependable people** — capable professionals who can become an extension of the client's organization.
2. **Reliable partnership** — Beepa remains involved after placement and supports the relationship.
3. **Operational visibility** — clients gain confidence through clear attendance, timesheets, reporting, requests, and communication.
4. **Flexible growth** — teams and workflows can evolve as client needs change.
5. **Human support** — employees and clients are treated as people, not account numbers.

---

# 4. Brand Pillars

## People

Communicate opportunity, respect, belonging, professional growth, and collaboration.

## Partnership

Communicate transparency, reliability, communication, accountability, and long-term working relationships.

## Progress

Communicate capacity, improvement, employee growth, client growth, and sustainable business growth.

## Purpose

Beepa has a real founding story. Use it authentically and with restraint. Do not exploit the story as sentimental advertising.

---

# 5. Core Brand Messages

## Primary Corporate Line

# People. Partnership. Progress.

Use for corporate branding, presentations, social profiles, footer branding, and high-level company communication.

## Brand Story Line

# Built on Purpose. Growing Together.

Use for the About page, careers, culture, anniversary content, employer branding, and founder-story communication.

## Marketing Message

# Better Teams. Stronger Businesses.

Use selectively on service pages, campaign pages, sales materials, and conversion-oriented messaging.

Do not place all three statements on the same section or design.

# 15. Graphic Language

Use the Beepa mark as visual DNA.

Primary devices:

1. **Interlocking Frames** — image frames, content framing, relationship diagrams.
2. **Cropped Curves** — large-scale section accents and composition anchors.
3. **Connected Lines** — subtle process or relationship graphics.
4. **Brand Pattern** — simplified mark geometry at approximately 3–8% opacity.

Never use all devices together in one section.

---

# 16. UI Style

Prefer:

- White and neutral surfaces
- Navy hierarchy
- Green actions and active states
- Lime micro-highlights
- 8px-based spacing
- Subtle borders
- Low-elevation shadows
- Medium corner radii
- Strong content hierarchy
- Consistent icon family

Avoid:

- Bubble UI
- Huge pills everywhere
- Neon gradients
- Excessive glassmorphism
- Heavy shadows
- Decorative charts
- Generic shadcn default styling

---

# 17. Motion

Motion communicates **connection and progress**.

Use GSAP/ScrollTrigger only for high-value storytelling moments:

- Founder timeline
- Connected-growth brand sequence
- Selected service stories
- Company-growth milestones

Use lighter CSS or component transitions for product microinteractions.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

# 18. Experience Principles

## Client Website

The user should think:

1. This looks professional.
2. They understand people and outsourcing.
3. They feel credible and transparent.
4. Their approach is differentiated.
5. I want to speak with them.

## Employee PWA

The user should immediately know:

1. What is my schedule?
2. Am I clocked in?
3. What needs my attention?
4. How much leave do I have?
5. Where is my payroll/payslip?

## Client Portal

The user should immediately know:

1. What is happening with my team?
2. What requires my approval?
3. Where are my reports and requests?
4. How do I contact Beepa?

## Admin

The user should immediately know:

1. What requires action today?
2. What is abnormal?
3. What is waiting for approval?
4. What trend matters?

---

# 19. Non-Negotiable Design Rules

1. Do not make Beepa look like a generic SaaS company.
2. Do not make Beepa look like a traditional call center.
3. Do not use all brand colors equally.
4. Do not overuse lime.
5. Do not use decorative gradients unless they are extremely restrained and brand-derived.
6. Do not use arbitrary colors as decoration.
7. Do not place every piece of information inside a card.
8. Do not use icons when typography or hierarchy is clearer.
9. Do not use motion merely to make the site look expensive.
10. Do not copy a component library's default visual identity.
11. Do not treat mobile as a shrunken desktop.
12. Do not expose client or employee data across tenants.
13. Do not let role-specific administration replace employee self-service; internal admins are employees too.
14. Accessibility is part of the design system, not a final QA phase.
15. Content clarity takes priority over decoration.

---

# 20. Final Approval Question

Before approving any Beepa output, ask:

- Does this feel credible to an international client?
- Does this still feel human?
- Is the information immediately understandable?
- Does it look unmistakably Beepa?
- Is it scalable across website, PWA, admin, careers, and marketing?
- Is the design helping the content rather than distracting from it?

If any answer is no, revise it.

# Beepa BPO — Brand Strategy

# 1. Brand Context

Beepa is a growing Philippine outsourcing company founded in 2019. Its identity must support international client acquisition, recruitment, a future workforce of roughly 100+ employees, and a proprietary operations platform without presenting the business as larger than it truly is.

The brand opportunity is to occupy the space between traditional BPOs and impersonal staffing marketplaces: **a credible, people-first partner with transparent operations and a strong relationship model**.

---

# 2. Brand Purpose

> Create better opportunities for people while helping businesses build stronger futures.

---

# 3. Vision

Build Beepa into a strong, sustainable, trusted outsourcing partner known for both the quality of its work and the way it treats its people and clients.

---

# 4. Mission Framework

Beepa should:

- Connect businesses with dependable Filipino professionals.
- Support long-term client-team relationships.
- Provide operational visibility and structured support.
- Create meaningful professional opportunities.
- Grow without sacrificing personal relationships and trust.

---

# 5. Positioning

## Category

People-first outsourcing partner.

## Positioning Statement

> **Beepa is a people-first outsourcing partner helping businesses build dependable teams and grow with confidence.**

## Differentiation

Beepa should differentiate on:

- Relationship quality
- Visibility
- Flexibility
- Human support
- Talent quality
- Operational structure
- Long-term partnership

Not on:

- Cheapest price
- Largest headcount
- AI hype
- Generic “24/7 BPO” claims

---

# 6. Audience Architecture

## Primary Client Audience

Business owners and operational decision-makers in markets such as the United States who need reliable outsourced teams.

Likely decision-makers:

- Founder / CEO
- COO
- Operations leader
- HR leader
- Department manager
- Finance/accounting leader
- Sales leader
- E-commerce operator

### Their Questions

- Can I trust this company?
- Will the people be capable?
- How much visibility will I have?
- How quickly can we build a team?
- What happens if there is a problem?
- Will Beepa remain involved after hiring?

## Candidate Audience

Filipino professionals looking for meaningful, stable work and professional growth.

### Their Questions

- Is this a legitimate company?
- Will I be treated well?
- Is there career growth?
- What roles are available?
- Is the application process clear?

## Existing Employee Audience

Employees need a clear, respectful self-service experience for attendance, leave, payroll, schedules, HR requests, and professional growth.

## Existing Client Audience

Clients need visibility, speed, confidence, and fewer fragmented communications.

---

# 7. Brand Pillars

## People

**Meaning:** Good businesses are powered by good people.

**Proof types:** Employee stories, training, real team imagery, retention, professional development, career opportunities.

## Partnership

**Meaning:** Beepa should operate as an extension of the client's organization.

**Proof types:** Partnership duration, case studies, client quotes, account management, client portal, responsiveness.

## Progress

**Meaning:** Outsourcing should create more capacity and better outcomes.

**Proof types:** Growth stories, improved workflows, team expansion, visibility, performance improvements.

## Purpose

**Meaning:** Beepa was built for a meaningful reason and remains grounded in human impact.

**Proof types:** Founder story, company history, family legacy, employee opportunity stories.

---

# 8. Message Hierarchy

## Level 1 — Brand

**People. Partnership. Progress.**

## Level 2 — Customer Value

**Better Teams. Stronger Businesses.**

## Level 3 — Story

**Built on Purpose. Growing Together.**

## Level 4 — Product Differentiation

Examples:

- Your team. Clearer visibility.
- One place to stay connected with your outsourced workforce.
- Flexible support built around how your business works.

---

# 9. Brand Voice

Beepa communicates like a capable partner, not a salesperson.

## Clear

Use straightforward language.

## Confident

State capabilities without exaggerated claims.

## Human

Use people-centered language.

## Approachable

Professional but not stiff.

## Purposeful

Every sentence should explain how Beepa helps, why Beepa can be trusted, or what opportunity Beepa creates.

---

# 10. Preferred Vocabulary

Use:

- Team
- Team members
- Professionals
- Specialists
- Talent
- Partner
- Client
- Support
- Opportunity
- Growth
- Visibility
- Flexible
- Dependable
- Reliable

Use carefully or avoid:

- Resources
- Seats
- Cheap labor
- Headcount as a people descriptor
- Disrupt
- Revolutionary
- World-class unless objectively supportable
- Best-in-class unless objectively supportable
- AI-powered as a generic marketing claim

---

# 11. Proof Before Claims

Beepa should avoid generic claims like:

> We provide world-class outsourcing solutions.

Prefer proof-led communication:

> Build a dedicated team with clear attendance, timesheets, support requests, and direct visibility in one Beepa workspace.

Proof can include:

- Founded 2019
- Verified client relationships
- Partnership duration
- Team growth
- Client-approved testimonials
- Case-study outcomes
- Real employees
- Operational platform capabilities

Never fabricate client logos, metrics, awards, testimonials, or performance results.

---

# 12. Brand Story Structure

The Beepa story should follow:

1. **Origin** — a family vision and purpose.
2. **Growth** — the purpose expanded beyond one family.
3. **Today** — helping professionals and businesses grow together.
4. **Future** — a sustainable, trusted, people-first outsourcing partner.

Tone: warm, dignified, concise, authentic.

Do not make grief the centerpiece of commercial messaging. The founder's legacy should be honored, not exploited.

---

# 13. Employer Brand

## Employer Promise

> Build your career with people who value your growth.

Communicate:

- Respect
- Opportunity
- Professional growth
- Clear processes
- Stability
- Community
- Meaningful client work

Do not reduce employer messaging to remote work, salary, perks, or “fun culture.”

---

# 14. Brand Architecture

Use one Beepa master brand.

Recommended expressions:

- Beepa BPO — corporate company identity
- Beepa Careers — recruitment experience, not a separate logo
- Beepa Platform / Beepa Workspace — client and operations software naming if needed

Do not create independent sub-brand logos for CRM, HR, payroll, attendance, or ticketing. They are modules of the Beepa system.

---

# 15. Brand Growth Rule

Beepa should look prepared for growth without pretending to already be a multinational enterprise.

Desired impression:

> **Credible. Growing. Human.**

Public site target:

- 25% navy
- 10% green
- 5% lime

Rules:

- Navy carries authority.
- Green carries identity and action.
- Lime carries emphasis only.
- Never give Navy, Green, and Lime equal visual weight.
- Do not introduce decorative purple, orange, cyan, or random gradient palettes.
- Semantic status colors may be used in product UI when meaning requires them.

---

# 4. Color Usage

## Navy

Use for:

- Navigation
- Major headings
- Footer
- Editorial dark sections
- Dashboard sidebar
- High-emphasis statistics

Avoid using Navy as the background of every section.

## Green

Use for:

- Primary CTA
- Active state
- Link emphasis
- Selected icons
- Progress/brand callouts

Avoid using Green for every heading.

## Lime

Use for:

- Small badges
- Tiny line accents
- Selected chart emphasis
- Decorative micro-elements

Never use Lime for long body text on white.

---

# 5. Typography

## Primary — Manrope

Role: display and brand expression.

Weights:

- 700: major headlines
- 600: section headings
- 500: buttons/navigation

## Secondary — Inter

Role: reading and UI.

Weights:

- 600: labels/emphasis
- 500: small headings
- 400: body text

---

# 6. Type Scale

Use fluid sizing with `clamp()`.

Suggested ranges:

| Style | Mobile | Desktop |
|---|---:|---:|
| Display | 48px | 72px |
| H1 | 40px | 56px |
| H2 | 32px | 44px |
| H3 | 24px | 32px |
| H4 | 20px | 24px |
| Body XL | 18px | 20px |
| Body | 16px | 18px |
| Small | 14px | 14px |
| Caption | 12px | 12px |

Headlines: tight line-height and tracking.  
Body: comfortable line-height and readable measure.

Recommended body-copy width: approximately 60–75 characters per line.

---

# 7. Spacing

Use an 8px-centered spacing scale:

`4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64 / 80 / 96 / 128`

Avoid arbitrary spacing unless needed for optical correction.

---

# 8. Grid

## Website

- 12-column desktop
- 8-column tablet
- 4-column mobile
- Typical content max: 1280–1440px
- Text-heavy editorial areas should be narrower.

## Product

Use responsive grid and container queries. Components should adapt to their available space, not only viewport width.

---

# 9. Corner Radius

Suggested tokens:

- Control: 8px
- Button: 8–10px
- Card: 12–16px
- Marketing media frame: 16–24px

Avoid 30–50px rounding on every surface.

---

# 10. Borders and Shadows

Prefer:

- 1px subtle border
- low-elevation shadow
- surface contrast
- spacing-based hierarchy

Avoid:

- heavy dark shadows
- neon glow
- green glow
- universal drop shadows

---

# 11. Logo-Derived Graphic Language

## Interlocking Frames

Two overlapping forms inspired by the Beepa mark.

Use for:

- Hero photo crops
- Testimonial framing
- Employer-brand imagery
- Presentation covers
- Partnership graphics

## Cropped Curves

Large portions of the symbol may extend beyond the canvas.

Use for:

- Hero background accents
- Section transitions
- Footer details

## Connected Lines

Use sparingly in process diagrams and relationship storytelling.

## Brand Pattern

Simplified mark geometry at 3–8% opacity.

Never place patterns behind important body copy when readability is reduced.

---

# 12. Iconography

Use one consistent outline icon system.

Direction:

- Approximately 2px stroke
- Rounded joins
- Minimal geometry
- Consistent visual weight
- No illustrative/cartoon icons in product UI

Color:

- Navy default
- Green secondary
- Semantic colors only for status meaning

Do not mix 3D icons, filled icons, outline icons, emoji, and illustrations.

---

# 13. Illustration

Beepa does not require a large illustration system.

If illustration is used:

- Keep it geometric and abstract.
- Derive forms from the logo.
- Keep color restrained.
- Use it to explain systems or relationships, not as decoration.

Avoid cartoon office scenes and generic AI-generated business illustrations.

---

# 14. Layout Style

Beepa layouts should use:

- Strong hierarchy
- Intentional whitespace
- Asymmetry where appropriate
- Editorial image composition
- Alternating dense and open sections
- Clear section rhythm

Do not place every section in a centered heading + three-card grid pattern.

---

# 15. Dark Mode

## Marketing Site

Light mode is primary. Dark mode is not required as the default brand expression.

## Application

Optional dark mode may use deep navy/ink surfaces rather than pure black.

Keep green as the active accent. Use lime sparingly.

# 11. Forms

Use progressive disclosure when forms are long.

- Name
- Work email
- Company
- Role
- Team/service need

# 12. Card Rules

Good:

- Statistic
- Testimonial
- Job opening
- Case study

Bad:

- Every section title
- Every icon
- Every navigation item

Avoid “card soup.”

---

# 13. Motion

Use high-value motion only:

- Hero image reveal
- Logo-derived mask transition
- Founder timeline
- Connected-growth section
- Count-up proof where meaningful
- Selected product mockup interactions

Do not pin five consecutive sections or animate every word.

# Beepa BPO — Responsive & Accessibility Standard

# 1. Principle

Beepa must be usable across phones, tablets, laptops, desktops, and large displays.

Responsive design is not “desktop squeezed smaller.” Each role's task must be intentionally designed for its context.

---

# 2. Mobile Priority

Mobile is especially important for:

- Employee attendance
- Push notifications
- Schedule
- Leave
- PTO/UPTO
- Cash advances
- Payslips
- Attendance corrections
- Approvals

Use:

- Bottom navigation where appropriate
- Bottom sheets
- Sticky primary actions
- Large tap targets
- Short forms
- Clear current status

---

# 3. Tablet Priority

Tablet should strongly support:

- Team leads
- HR reviewers
- Operations
- Client monitoring
- Approval workflows

Layouts may use two-column structures where space permits.

---

# 4. Desktop Priority

Desktop is primary for:

- Payroll
- CRM
- Recruitment
- Reporting
- Website CMS
- Finance
- Complex HR operations
- Settings

Use:

- Sidebar navigation
- Dense but readable tables
- Split views
- Filter panels
- Bulk actions
- Keyboard-friendly workflows

---

# 5. Orientation

Support portrait and landscape intentionally.

Do not rely on a single device width.

Landscape mobile/tablet should not produce broken headers, clipped modals, or unusable fixed heights.

---

# 6. Safe Areas

PWA UI must respect device safe areas:

- iPhone Dynamic Island/notch
- Bottom home indicator
- Android cutouts
- Installed PWA browser chrome changes

Use `env(safe-area-inset-*)` where appropriate.

Do not place critical controls flush against unsafe screen edges.

---

# 7. Fluid Sizing

Prefer:

- `clamp()` for type/spacing where useful
- CSS Grid
- Flexbox
- Container queries
- intrinsic sizing
- `minmax()`

Avoid large collections of arbitrary device-specific breakpoints.

---

# 8. Touch Targets

Interactive controls must be comfortably tappable.

Do not use tiny icon-only actions without adequate hit area or accessible label.

Critical employee actions such as Clock In/Out require prominent tap targets.

---

# 9. Accessibility Principles

Accessibility is a design requirement.

Minimum expectations:

- Semantic HTML
- Keyboard support
- Visible focus
- Logical tab order
- Screen-reader labels
- Strong contrast
- Form labels
- Error descriptions
- Accessible dialogs
- Accessible tables
- Reduced motion
- Non-color status cues

---

# 10. Color Accessibility

Never rely on color alone.

Examples:

- Late = color + “Late” label
- Approved = color + “Approved” label
- Error = icon + error text + field association

Lime must not be used for low-contrast body copy.

---

# 11. Keyboard Navigation

Desktop admin workflows must support keyboard use.

Ensure:

- Focus visible
- Dialog focus trapped correctly
- Escape closes overlays when appropriate
- Menus and comboboxes behave predictably
- Table actions are keyboard reachable

---

# 12. Forms

Every input needs a real label.

Placeholders are examples, not labels.

Errors should be adjacent to the relevant field and understandable without color alone.

---

# 13. Motion Accessibility

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

When enabled:

- Remove decorative parallax
- Disable scrubbed/pinned story motion where needed
- Reduce large transform animations
- Keep essential state transitions simple

Content must never depend on motion to be understood.

---

# 14. Responsive Table Strategy

Do not force every table into horizontal scrolling.

Depending on task:

- Hide low-priority columns
- Allow column customization
- Use expandable rows
- Convert to list cards on mobile
- Keep high-value data visible

---

# 15. Modal Strategy

Desktop:

- Dialog for moderate tasks
- Side panel for contextual editing

Mobile:

- Full-height sheet or page for complex workflows
- Bottom sheet for simple selections/actions

Avoid tiny desktop dialogs squeezed onto phones.

# 10. Anti-Drift Rule

When a designer or AI proposes something “more modern,” reject it if it introduces unnecessary:

- New colors
- New fonts
- New border radius language
- New icon style
- New gradient language
- New card style
- New animation behavior

Modernization should improve clarity, accessibility, composition, performance, or usability—not add novelty.
